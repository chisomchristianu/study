# Ceramics

#materials-science #concept

Source: [lecture, slides 27, 63](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

| Focus | Scope |
|---|---|
| Ceramics | Lecture concepts plus labelled explanation |

Supplement: engineering ceramics are commonly inorganic, non-metallic materials with strong ionic/covalent bonding. Many have high hardness, high temperature capability and good wear resistance, but low tensile ductility. Examples include alumina and silicon carbide.

> [!warning] Conditions and limits
> Flaws, porosity, loading mode and thermal shock control performance. High compressive strength does not imply high tensile toughness; some ceramics are conductive.
