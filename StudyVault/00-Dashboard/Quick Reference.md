# Quick Reference

#materials-science #dashboard

Source: [lecture, slides all](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

## Climate and Service Life → [[Climate and Service Life]]

Hot rail: check thermal restraint and buckling, not just room-temperature strength.

## Carbon Footprint and Material Selection → [[Carbon Footprint and Material Selection]]

Compare materials by functional unit, not just kg CO₂e/kg.

## Circular Economy → [[Circular Economy]]

A recyclable constituent does not guarantee a recyclable assembly.

## Social Impacts and Policy → [[Social Impacts and Policy]]

Sustainability includes supply-chain conditions as well as emissions.

## Ferrous Metals → [[Ferrous Metals]]

Identify iron base, then carbon content, microstructure and processing.

## Non-ferrous Metals → [[Non-ferrous Metals]]

Electrical conductor: copper; very low-density metal: magnesium.

## Polymers → [[Polymers]]

A room-temperature modulus is insufficient for a hot, continuously loaded polymer part.

## Ceramics → [[Ceramics]]

Hardness and heat resistance can coexist with brittle fracture.

## Glasses → [[Glasses]]

Distinguish amorphous glass from crystalline ceramic structure.

## Fibre Composites → [[Fibre Composites]]

Always specify fibre direction when quoting stiffness or expansion.

## Mechanical Properties Overview → [[Mechanical Properties Overview]]

Identify the failure mechanism before selecting a property.

## Thermal Properties → [[Thermal Properties]]

Do not confuse insulation with heat storage.

## Electrical and Magnetic Properties → [[Electrical and Magnetic Properties]]

Choose electrical resistance from both material resistivity and geometry.

## Chemical Properties → [[Chemical Properties]]

Assess material + environment + stress together.

## Technological Properties → [[Technological Properties]]

Specify the manufacturing route before judging suitability.

## Ecological Properties → [[Ecological Properties]]

Assess equivalent service delivered across the whole life cycle.

## Social Properties → [[Social Properties]]

A technically suitable and low-carbon material can still have social risks.

## Stress Strain and Thermal Expansion → [[Stress Strain and Thermal Expansion]]

Ask whether expansion is free or restrained before calculating stress.

## Material Symmetry → [[Material Symmetry]]

Do not apply isotropic modulus relationships blindly to composites.

## Elasticity and Elastic Moduli → [[Elasticity and Elastic Moduli]]

Use E for axial strain, G for shear and K for volume change.

## Component Stiffness → [[Component Stiffness]]

A higher E does not guarantee a stiffer component if geometry changes.

## Viscosity Viscoelasticity and Creep → [[Viscosity Viscoelasticity and Creep]]

Distinguish constant-stress creep from constant-strain relaxation.

## Hardness → [[Hardness]]

A very hard material can still fracture in a brittle manner.

## Strength and Plasticity → [[Strength and Plasticity]]

Strength sets a failure threshold; stiffness sets elastic deformation.

## Ductility → [[Ductility]]

Use cross-sectional areas for Z, not lengths.

## Toughness and True Stress → [[Toughness and True Stress]]

A steep initial slope measures E, not the total area to fracture.

## Must-know formulas → [[Stress Strain and Thermal Expansion]]

| Formula | Conditions |
|---|---|
| σ=F/A₀; ε=ΔL/L₀ | Engineering definitions |
| εth=αΔT; ΔL=αL₀ΔT | Uniform temperature, constant α |
| σth=−EαΔT | Fully restrained 1D elastic bar |

## Elastic and component formulas → [[Elasticity and Elastic Moduli]]

σ=Eε; τ=Gγ; ν=−εtrans/εaxial; p=−KΔV/V. Isotropic linear elastic: E=2G(1+ν), K=E/[3(1−2ν)].

EA/L = axial spring stiffness; EI = bending rigidity; GJ = torsional rigidity; GJ/L = shaft torsional spring stiffness. See [[Component Stiffness]].

## Plasticity and energy formulas → [[Toughness and True Stress]]

Z=(A₀−Af)/A₀×100% (see [[Ductility]]). σtrue=F/Acurrent; εtrue=ln(L/L₀). Before necking with uniform plastic deformation: σtrue=σeng(1+εeng). U=∫σeng dεeng up to fracture.

## Transport and emissions → [[Electrical and Magnetic Properties]]

R=ρe L/A; scalar σe=1/ρe. Linear B=μH. Emissions=mass×emission factor; see [[Carbon Footprint and Material Selection]].

## Viscous flow → [[Viscosity Viscoelasticity and Creep]]

Ideal dashpot: σ=η dε/dt.
