# Exam Traps

#materials-science #dashboard

Source: [lecture, slides all](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

## Climate and Service Life

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Temperature trends and event statistics are lecture claims, not independently updated measurements. A single year above 1.5 °C is not the same as a sustained breach of the Paris long-term temperature goal.
> [[Climate and Service Life]]

## Carbon Footprint and Material Selection

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Compare equivalent functions and lifetimes. Electricity mix, allocation, processing, transport and system boundaries affect results; low emissions per kg do not alone identify the best component.
> [[Carbon Footprint and Material Selection]]

## Circular Economy

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Supplement: open-loop recycling means a different product system and does not always imply downcycling. Actual recyclability also depends on collection, contamination, economics and available infrastructure.
> [[Circular Economy]]

## Social Impacts and Policy

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> These are slide summaries, not verified current legal guidance. Scope, implementation dates and exemptions must be checked against official sources. Risks differ between mines and suppliers.
> [[Social Impacts and Policy]]

## Ferrous Metals

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Cast iron is not uniformly brittle: graphite morphology matters, especially the distinction between flake and nodular graphite. Heat treatment and microstructure strongly affect both families.
> [[Ferrous Metals]]

## Non-ferrous Metals

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> High conductivity is not the same as high strength. Heat resistance is relative to alloy, environment and duty; magnesium corrosion and fire considerations depend on form and application.
> [[Non-ferrous Metals]]

## Polymers

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Glass transition concerns amorphous phases and is not melting. Grade, reinforcement, moisture and temperature alter behaviour; not every polymer fits the indicative ranges.
> [[Polymers]]

## Ceramics

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Flaws, porosity, loading mode and thermal shock control performance. High compressive strength does not imply high tensile toughness; some ceramics are conductive.
> [[Ceramics]]

## Glasses

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Surface flaws dominate practical tensile strength. Tempering can introduce beneficial surface compression; transparency is not universal.
> [[Glasses]]

## Fibre Composites

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Properties transverse to fibres can differ greatly. Delamination, matrix softening, impact damage and recycling difficulty may limit use. Near-zero axial expansion is lay-up dependent.
> [[Fibre Composites]]

## Mechanical Properties Overview

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> No single property ranks a material for every application. Fatigue and wear data require test conditions and geometry.
> [[Mechanical Properties Overview]]

## Thermal Properties

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Properties vary with temperature, direction and phase. Celsius temperature differences equal kelvin differences, but absolute-temperature equations require kelvin.
> [[Thermal Properties]]

## Electrical and Magnetic Properties

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Conductivity and permeability may be tensors, nonlinear or frequency-dependent. Supplement: diamagnetism is a weak negative response, completing the basic comparison.
> [[Electrical and Magnetic Properties]]

## Chemical Properties

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Corrosion-resistant does not mean immune. Passivating alloys may suffer localized corrosion. The specific bridge-cause attribution is a lecture statement, not an independently reviewed investigation here.
> [[Chemical Properties]]

## Technological Properties

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Hardenability is not the same as final hardness. These are process-dependent assessments, not universal material constants.
> [[Technological Properties]]

## Ecological Properties

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Carbon footprint is one impact category; water, toxicity, land use and end-of-life losses may change the decision. Recycling credits depend on accounting assumptions.
> [[Ecological Properties]]

## Social Properties

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Country or material labels alone do not establish conditions at a specific operation. Compliance paperwork and actual outcomes should be distinguished.
> [[Social Properties]]

## Stress Strain and Thermal Expansion

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Free, uniform expansion creates no stress. Partial restraint, thermal gradients, yielding or buckling change the fully restrained estimate. N/mm² = MPa.
> [[Stress Strain and Thermal Expansion]]

## Material Symmetry

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Counts assume classical 3D linear elasticity with usual energy symmetry. A polycrystal may be approximately isotropic at one scale but textured at another.
> [[Material Symmetry]]

## Elasticity and Elastic Moduli

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Elastic does not always mean linear. Near-incompressible behaviour makes K large; heterogeneous or anisotropic apparent Poisson ratios need different bounds. Lecture tabulated values are approximate and not all rows satisfy isotropic relations exactly.
> [[Elasticity and Elastic Moduli]]

## Component Stiffness

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Rigidity and spring stiffness have different units. For noncircular sections J is a torsion constant, generally not the polar area moment.
> [[Component Stiffness]]

## Viscosity Viscoelasticity and Creep

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Not every viscoelastic model fully recovers: the Maxwell model can retain dashpot strain. Creep may involve viscoplastic mechanisms and permanent deformation. Loading-rate rules are qualitative and material-dependent.
> [[Viscosity Viscoelasticity and Creep]]

## Hardness

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Hardness is not toughness or a universal wear predictor. Conversions to tensile strength are empirical and material-specific; compare scales and test conditions carefully.
> [[Hardness]]

## Strength and Plasticity

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Some materials have no clear yield point; a proof stress such as Rp0.2 may be used. Strength depends on temperature, rate and loading mode. Ultimate engineering stress is not generally the fracture stress.
> [[Strength and Plasticity]]

## Ductility

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> These ranges are teaching heuristics, not universal classification standards. Not every failure shows visible warning. Ductility alone does not determine absorbed energy.
> [[Ductility]]

## Toughness and True Stress

> [!danger]- Check the assumption
> Tensile toughness U is distinct from fracture toughness KIC (MPa√m) and impact energy (J). After necking, local strain and multiaxial stress require care. Published toughness values depend strongly on grade and test conditions.
> [[Toughness and True Stress]]
