# Viscosity Viscoelasticity and Creep

#materials-science #concept

Source: [lecture, slides 16, 47, 56–57](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

Supplement:

| Model or behaviour | Response |
|---|---|
| Ideal spring | σ = Eε; immediate recovery |
| Ideal dashpot | σ = η dε/dt; permanent flow |
| Viscoelastic solid | Time-dependent strain and delayed recovery |
| Creep test | Hold stress; observe increasing strain |
| Stress relaxation | Hold strain; observe decreasing stress |

Springs and dashpots model recoverable and dissipative contributions. Fast loading often appears stiffer; slow loading permits relaxation or flow. Heating often lowers viscosity.

> [!warning] Conditions and limits
> Not every viscoelastic model fully recovers: the Maxwell model can retain dashpot strain. Creep may involve viscoplastic mechanisms and permanent deformation. Loading-rate rules are qualitative and material-dependent.
