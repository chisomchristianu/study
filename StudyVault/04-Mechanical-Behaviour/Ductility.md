# Ductility

#materials-science #concept

Source: [lecture, slides 62–64](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

Reduction of area: Z = (A₀−Af)/A₀ ×100%. If A₀=100 mm² and Af=60 mm², Z=40%. Supplement: elongation at fracture is another ductility measure and depends on gauge length.

| Lecture Z range | Illustrative label |
|---|---|
| <5% | Brittle |
| 5% to <20% | Moderately ductile |
| 20% to <50% | Ductile |
| ≥50% | Highly ductile |

Plastic deformation can support forming and provide visible necking before tensile fracture.

> [!warning] Conditions and limits
> These ranges are teaching heuristics, not universal classification standards. Not every failure shows visible warning. Ductility alone does not determine absorbed energy.
