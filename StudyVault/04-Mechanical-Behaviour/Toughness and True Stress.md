# Toughness and True Stress

#materials-science #concept

Source: [lecture, slides 65–68](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

Standard textbook reconstruction:

| Quantity | Expression |
|---|---|
| True stress | σtrue = F/Acurrent |
| True strain | εtrue = ln(L/L₀) |
| Engineering tensile toughness | U = ∫₀^εf σeng dεeng |

The area under a tensile stress–strain curve is energy per original volume: Pa = J/m³. For uniform deformation before necking, σtrue = σeng(1+εeng), εtrue = ln(1+εeng). A high stress capacity combined with significant strain can produce large absorbed energy. Lecture examples range from glass ≈0.01 to structural steel ≈100–200 MJ/m³.

> [!warning] Conditions and limits
> Tensile toughness U is distinct from fracture toughness KIC (MPa√m) and impact energy (J). After necking, local strain and multiaxial stress require care. Published toughness values depend strongly on grade and test conditions.
