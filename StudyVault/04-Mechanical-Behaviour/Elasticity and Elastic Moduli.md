# Elasticity and Elastic Moduli

#materials-science #concept

Source: [lecture, slides 47, 49–54](https://cwillberg.github.io/Lectures/stream_material_science/stream_wst_01/). Explanatory additions are identified as supplements.

Standard textbook equations supplementing the SVG formulas:

| Response | Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Axial | σ = Eε | Young modulus E |
| Lateral contraction | ν = −εtrans/εaxial | Poisson ratio |
| Shear | τ = Gγ | Shear modulus G |
| Hydrostatic compression | p = −K ΔV/V | Bulk modulus K |
| Isotropic relationship | E = 2G(1+ν) | Linear isotropic elasticity |
| Isotropic relationship | K = E/[3(1−2ν)] | Linear isotropic elasticity |

For steel E≈200 GPa, ν≈0.30 gives G≈76.9 GPa. For stable isotropic linear elasticity −1<ν<0.5; many ordinary solids have positive ν. Ideal elastic strain recovers on unloading.

> [!warning] Conditions and limits
> Elastic does not always mean linear. Near-incompressible behaviour makes K large; heterogeneous or anisotropic apparent Poisson ratios need different bounds. Lecture tabulated values are approximate and not all rows satisfy isotropic relations exactly.

## Exam/Test Patterns

| Trigger | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| Typical distinction | Use E for axial strain, G for shear and K for volume change. |
