## Question 1 [recall]

> Define engineering stress and strain.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> σ = F/A₀; ε = (L−L₀)/L₀.

## Question 2 [recall]

> What is Young modulus?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The axial stress/strain slope in the linear elastic range.

## Question 3 [recall]

> What is Poisson ratio?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> ν = −εtrans/εaxial for the specified uniaxial loading direction.

## Question 4 [recall]

> What distinguishes isotropy and orthotropy?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Isotropy is direction independent; orthotropy has three orthogonal material symmetry planes.

## Question 5 [recall]

> Define reduction of area.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Z = (A₀−Af)/A₀ ×100%.

## Question 6 [recall]

> What does area under an engineering tensile stress–strain curve represent?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Absorbed energy per original volume up to the chosen strain, and tensile toughness when integrated to fracture.

## Question 7 [application]

> A 100 mm² bar carries 20 kN. Find engineering stress.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> 20,000 N / 100 mm² = 200 MPa.

## Question 8 [application]

> A free 1 m steel bar heats by 50 K with α=12×10⁻⁶/K. Find extension.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> ΔL=αLΔT=0.0006 m=0.6 mm; free uniform expansion creates no thermal stress.

## Question 9 [analysis]

> Two bars have equal E and area but different length. Which is axially stiffer?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The shorter bar: k=EA/L. Material modulus alone does not determine component stiffness.

## Question 10 [analysis]

> A hard, high-strength specimen fractures at tiny strain. Must it be tough?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> No. Toughness depends on energy absorbed to fracture; high stress with very little strain can give low toughness.
